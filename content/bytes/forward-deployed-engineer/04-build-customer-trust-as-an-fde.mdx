## Quick Start

An FDE may understand the technology and still fail if the customer does not trust the delivery process. On day one at a client site, you are a stranger. This standalone Byte explains how discovery, honest communication, visible evidence and reliable follow-through build trust.

**Time investment:** 20-25 mins.

## Winning Back a Skeptical Customer

Rahul's first solo engagement is with an insurance company client. In the first meeting, the client's tech lead says bluntly: "We tried another vendor for this before and it failed, so we're skeptical." Rahul feels a bit rattled.

He calls Divya: "They don't trust us yet — what do I do?" Divya tells him: "Rahul, don't expect trust on day one. The Umbrex playbook's core principle is: **'Build WITH the customer, not merely FOR the customer.'** Follow that, and trust will come naturally."

## Core Explanation

What does "Build WITH, not FOR" actually mean? Imagine hiring an interior designer. There are two kinds: Designer A never asks your opinion, designs the whole house based on their own idea, and says "here it is" at the end. Designer B asks your preference for every room, shows you sample options, and iterates based on your feedback.

Designer B's approach is "build WITH" — treating the client as a **participant**, not just a recipient. This matters a lot for FDEs, because the client's internal team understands their own systems far better than you do, and without their input, the solution won't work in the real world.

**Executive presence** is another skill here — speaking confidently, jargon-free, and business-outcome-focused when talking to senior stakeholders like a CFO or CTO.

**Founder-like ownership** — when a client's problem comes up, instead of saying "that's a different team's job" and stepping back, you take ownership: "this is my problem, I'll solve it."

## Inside the Workflow

Per the Umbrex playbook, FDEs need to communicate effectively across every level — executives, operators, engineers, and frontline users. The language shifts for each audience:

- **With executives** — talk business outcomes, ROI, and risk.
- **With operators/mid-managers** — talk process change, adoption, and training.
- **With engineers** — talk technical architecture, APIs, and integration details.
- **With frontline users** — talk simply about "how this makes your daily work easier."

It's a bit like code-switching — different "language" for different audiences, while the core message stays the same.

**Fast-tracking trust:** the Umbrex playbook suggests delivering a small, visible win (a proof-of-value) within the first 1-2 weeks. Instead of making big promises, reliably delivering a small commitment fast builds trust exponentially.

## Practical Example

Rahul followed Divya's advice. Instead of making big promises in the first 2 weeks, he focused on just one pain point the client mentioned: "Our policy renewal reminders are manual — can we automate that?" He built it small, and demoed it by week 2. The client's tech lead reacted: "This is exactly the problem we wanted solved!" — trust started building from there.

After that, in every meeting, Rahul would ask the client's team "what should our next step be?" instead of pushing his own decision — deciding together, rather than forcing it.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Forcing your own idea without consulting the client** — falling into the "Build FOR" trap.
2. **Making big promises you can't deliver on** — once trust breaks, it's hard to rebuild.
3. **Using the same language for every audience** — technical jargon with a CFO creates disconnect.
4. **Not taking ownership, deflecting with "that's not our job"** — this erodes client trust.
5. **Proposing a big, complex solution in the very first interaction** — win small first, then build bigger trust.

## Compare and Contrast

| Approach | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Build FOR (without client input) | Fast initially, but adoption/trust issues later |
| Build WITH (client as participant) | Slower at first, but stronger trust + adoption |
| Big promise, delayed delivery | Trust erosion |
| Small, fast, reliable wins | Trust compounds |

## Key Takeaways

- Core principle: **"Build WITH the customer, not merely FOR the customer"** — treat the client as a participant.
- Executive presence — adjust your language for the audience (executives/operators/engineers/frontline users).
- To build trust fast, deliver a small, reliable win within the first 1-2 weeks.
- Founder-like ownership — treat the client's problem as your own.
- Skip the big promises; consistent, small delivery is what compounds trust.

## FAQ and Knowledge Check

**Q1: What exactly does "Build WITH, not FOR" mean?**
Actively involving the client as a participant in the solution design process, rather than designing it entirely on your own without their input.

**Q2: What is executive presence?**
The ability to communicate confidently, jargon-free, and business-outcome-focused with senior stakeholders (CFO/CTO).

**Q3: What's the best strategy for building trust fast?**
Skip the big promises — deliver a small, visible, reliable win within the first 1-2 weeks.
